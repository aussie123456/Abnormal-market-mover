Tier 1 · Large caps moving abnormally

Market cap of US$10bn or more and today's move at least 3 standard deviations from the name's own 200-session daily-return distribution. These are the ones where the market's model was wrong.

Tier 2 · Moved 30% or more over five sessions

Any size in the universe. Revenue-generating operators only. Click a row for headlines and the balance sheet.

Tier 3 · Moved 10% or more today

Today's move versus the prior close, live while a market is open. Names already in tier 1 or tier 2 are listed again here so each list is complete on its own.

Set aside · Pre-revenue names that tripped a screen

Trailing revenue below US$50m. Explorers, clinical-stage biotech and pre-commercial names, where a big move is a punt repricing rather than a model breaking.

How the screen works

Universe. S&P 500 members, every NASDAQ-listed company with a market cap of US$1bn or more, the top of the ASX by market cap (about the ASX 300), Nikkei 225 members and the largest KOSPI names (about the KOSPI 200). Roughly 2,000 companies.

Stage filter. A name needs trailing twelve-month revenue of at least US$50m to appear in the tiers. Below that it is set aside and counted, not hidden.

Abnormality. z is today's log return minus the name's mean daily log return over the prior 200 sessions, divided by the standard deviation of those returns. Price versus the 200-day moving average is shown for context only.

Balance sheet. Net debt is total debt less cash. Net debt to EBITDA uses trailing EBITDA; "net cash" means cash exceeds debt. Debt to EV is total debt as a share of enterprise value. Facility-level detail (undrawn lines, converts, maturities) needs filings and is not on this page.

Refresh. Hourly on weekdays by a cloud routine. Prices and fundamentals are from Yahoo Finance; headlines are from Google News. Market caps and balance sheets are converted to US dollars at the run's spot rates. Notes are written by the routine from the headlines and are a prompt to look further, not a conclusion.
